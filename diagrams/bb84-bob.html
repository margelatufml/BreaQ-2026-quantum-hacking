BB84: Bob's Side

Turn 200,000 noisy, possibly eavesdropped qubits into a secret key that matches Alice's, or abort. Noise and an eavesdropper look identical, so every error is paid for as if Eve caused it.

Sent
200,000 qubits
Channel
3% noise, Eve on 1%
Error bound Q_u
3.86%
Final secret key
42,856 bits

The project at a glance

  1. quantumQubitsAlice sends 200,000 in random bases. Bob measures in his own random bases.
  2. classicalSiftKeep the positions where the bases match: 99,885 bits.
  3. checkTestSacrifice 20,000 bits to measure the error. Bound it: Q_u = 3.86%. Abort above 11%.
  4. classicalRepairCascade fixes every error, a hash confirms. It leaks 18,110 bits.
  5. outputKeySubtract what Eve may know and what leaked, then squeeze: 42,856 bits.

2 · Sifting on a toy example

12 qubits instead of 200,000. Basis 0 = Z, qubit is |0⟩ or |1⟩. Basis 1 = X, qubit is |+⟩ or |−⟩. Bob keeps a position only when the two bases are equal.

Read it

Kept positions: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, six of 12, about half. At each of them Alice's bit and Bob's result agree.

The dropped ones

With different bases Bob's result is a coin flip. Position 0 agrees by luck; positions 7, 8, 9 and 11 do not. That is why they are thrown away. In the real run 99,885 of 200,000 are kept.

5 · Why each choice

Clopper-Pearson, not Hoeffding

Both bounds hold except with probability 10⁻¹⁰. Clopper-Pearson is tighter: Q_u 3.86% against 5.42%. A tighter bound means less charged to Eve: 42,856 key bits against 37,411.

Back-tracking Cascade, not the brief's 24 / 24 / 24

Passes of 24, 72, 216 with back-tracking give 42,856 bits. The brief's literal three passes of 24 give 28,842, and the first hash check mismatched. A mismatch means another Cascade run, which leaks more parities.

Why 11%

The brief's abort threshold. Above about 11% the channel is too noisy or too eavesdropped for any secret key. 12% noise with no Eve already gives Q_u 13.22%, and the run aborts.

Why every error is charged to Eve

Bob cannot tell noise from an eavesdropper. So n·h(Q_u) = 18,852 bits, 23.6% of the 79,885, are assumed known to Eve and removed from the key.

Why the Toeplitz multiply uses an FFT

The matrix is 42,856 × 79,885 = 3,423,551,560 entries. It is a Toeplitz matrix, so T·x is a convolution of the seed with the key, and an FFT does it without building the matrix. The whole run takes 0.0624 s.

Why the hash bits count as leakage

The 40 hash bits come from Alice over the public channel, so Eve hears them too. leak_EC = 18,070 parity + 40 hash = 18,110. Alice's full ledger: 20,000 test + 18,070 parity + 40 hash = 38,110.

7 · Words you need

Sifting
Keeping only the positions where Alice's and Bob's bases are equal.
Basis
How a qubit is encoded or measured. Z gives |0⟩ or |1⟩; X gives |+⟩ or |−⟩.
QBER
Quantum bit error rate: the share of sifted bits where Bob and Alice disagree.
Test sample
k = 20,000 sifted bits both sides reveal to measure the error rate. They are then thrown away.
Clopper-Pearson
An exact worst-case upper bound on the true error rate from m errors in k tests.
Hoeffding
A looser bound: Q̂ + √(ln(1/ε) / 2k). Simpler, but it costs key bits.
Abort threshold
If Q_u is above 11%, discard everything; no key is issued.
Cascade
Error correction by comparing block parities and bisecting the blocks that differ.
Parity
Whether a block has an even or odd number of ones. One bit, and one leaked bit.
Back-tracking
After a fix, re-searching earlier passes whose blocks turned odd.
leak_EC
All bits revealed during error correction: parities plus the hash. Subtracted from the key.
Binary entropy h
h(x) = −x·log₂x − (1−x)·log₂(1−x): how many bits of uncertainty an error rate x hides.
Privacy amplification
Shrinking the corrected key to a shorter one Eve knows essentially nothing about.
Toeplitz matrix
A matrix with constant diagonals, so a short random seed defines all of it.
One-time pad
XOR a message with a secret key of the same length, used once.
Intercept-resend
Eve measures a qubit in a random basis and sends on her result. When she guesses the basis wrong she disturbs it.

8 · Predict a new case

The jury may change one thing and ask what happens. Our answers, all from verified runs:

  • Noise 2% instead of 3%?Q_u 2.76%, key 51,695 bits.
  • Noise 4%?Q_u 4.95%, key 34,472 bits.
  • A very clean channel, 1% noise?Q_u 1.74%, key 61,083 bits: 18,227 more than at 3%.
  • Eve intercepts half of the qubits?Q_u 16.21%, above 11%: the run aborts and no key is issued.
  • 12% noise and no Eve at all?Q_u 13.22%: abort. Noise alone is enough to stop the key.
  • Hoeffding instead of Clopper-Pearson?Key 37,411 instead of 42,856.
  • The brief's literal Cascade, 24 / 24 / 24?Key 28,842, and the first hash check mismatches.
  • The hash mismatches three times?The key is discarded: no key, no message.

9 · At the event

  1. Fill in OrganiserTransport once the organisers publish their API: routes and bit encodings.
  2. Plug in their pre-agreed hash.
  3. Check that Bob's key length matches the server's, and keep the hash-leak setting where they agree.

Limitations

  • Only a simulated Alice: the qubit transfer is a shortcut in alice_sim.py.
  • The organisers' API is not known yet; OrganiserTransport is an empty skeleton.
  • The hash is our own SHA-256 prefix and the Toeplitz seed layout is our own choice (diag).
  • The public channel is assumed to be authenticated; the brief does not cover authentication.

Say it to the jury

  1. We cannot tell noise from Eve, so we charge every error to Eve: that is why only 42,856 of 200,000 qubits become key.
  2. Every bit Alice and Bob reveal, test bits, parities and the hash, is counted and subtracted before privacy amplification.
  3. If the error bound passes 11% or the key length goes negative, we abort instead of issuing a key we cannot defend.
  4. Each choice was tested against its alternative: Hoeffding gives 37,411 bits, and the literal Cascade gives 28,842 and a hash mismatch.
